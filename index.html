Step 1 — Search Anxiety over Time

Public Query Patterns

Google Search is a private mirror of our public anxieties. By looking at search interest over the last 24 months, we can track exactly when and how much interest spikes for different health concerns.

Select a condition above to see national weekly and monthly trend variations. Notice seasonal spikes (like Dengue during the monsoon) versus chronic steady lines (like Diabetes and Hair Loss).

Step 2 — Search Intensity by State

Where is the Concern?

Geographical search interest (normalized to a 0–100 scale) shows where people search for this condition the most. High values don't represent absolute counts, but rather the proportion of total searches originating from that state.

States colored in deep green show high query concentrations. Urban hubs and highly literate states generally show a significantly higher search frequency.

Step 3 — Clinical Outcomes Map

What does the Data Say?

We map this against the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5) and related health reports. This represents the actual, clinical footprint of the condition at the granular district level (~641 administrative units).

Deep blue shades show higher disease burdens or clinical risk. In some areas, the burden is heavily concentrated in rural communities, while in others, it is an urban epidemic.

Step 4 — Syncing Search & Reality

Interactive Correlation

Hover on any state or district in either map. The matching unit in the corresponding map will highlight simultaneously, with tools showing their comparison.

Observe the visual overlay. If search patterns matched reality, the dark green regions on the left would perfectly line up with the dark blue regions on the right. Frequently, they do not.

Step 5 — The Gap Scatter Plot

Quantifying the Disconnect

By plotting search interest (X-axis) against actual health outcomes (Y-axis), we can statistically visualize the mismatch. States in the top-left have high disease burden but low search interest; states in the bottom-right search intensely despite lower clinical burden.

X: Search Interest (0-100) Y: Health Reality

Health of India

This project is an interactive data visualization exploring the gap between what Indians search for on the internet versus what is recorded in clinical health surveys. Search interest is compiled from Google Trends (2024-2025). Clinical outcome data is sourced from the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5, 2019-2021) and the India State-Level Disease Burden Initiative.

Built with Pure D3.js & HTML5/CSS3. Administrative boundaries courtesy of Survey of India and BharatLabs.
